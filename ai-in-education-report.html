What happens to learning outcomes when students are given access to ChatGPT?

Short answer: It depends entirely on how and when students use it.
AP
Aashish Panthi September 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Few days ago, I came across a viral video where a middle school student did his homework using AI tool. He used differentiation to solve simple mathematical solutions. A grade 7 student in Nepal isn't taught calculus so a genuine question arises, "Are students really learning when they have access to AI?"

Similarly, a article published on USC Today mentions that from a survey of 1,000 U.S. college students, they found that most use AI for seeking fast solutions with minimal effort. So, here is my answer to "Is ChatGPT helping students learn?"

Key Takeaways:

  • In a controlled experiment with almost 1,000 high-school maths students, researchers split them into three groups: one got a plain ChatGPT interface, one got a tutor version built to give hints instead of answers, and one got no AI. Both AI groups did far better during practice. Then everyone sat an exam with no AI. The plain-ChatGPT group scored 17% below the no-AI group. The tutor group showed no such drop. The authors describe the plain-AI use as a "crutch": lean on it to get through practice, and the skill never forms.
  • In an MIT Media Lab study, 54 participants wrote essays either with ChatGPT, with a search engine, or with nothing, while an EEG recorded their brain activity. The ChatGPT writers showed the weakest brain connectivity, recalled less of what they had just written, and produced more look-alike essays. Participants who wrote first on their own and only then brought in AI stayed sharper than people who relied for the model from the start.
  • A meta-analysis pooling 37 experiments found that, on average, ChatGPT has a moderately positive effect on academic achievement (Hedges' g of about 0.58). So it does help. How much depends on when in the process it shows up and what kind of task it is asked to do.

My summary

Performance and learning are two different things. Use of AI raises performance straight away, because it does parts of the work with students. Learning happens when we struggle. Like in the experiment, the plain-ChatGPT students looked strong in practice and then could not repeat it without ChatGPT, because the model was working for them, students didn't struggle. However, when AI's help is taken after you have already thought, the struggle stays and your learning holds, which is effective.

How to use AI so it actually helps you study

  • First attempt the problem yourself, then bring in AI to check your work. Like checking grammar and getting feedback. Supported by MIT study.
  • Ask it to explain the idea and to quiz you, not to hand over the finished answer. After getting help, close the chat and redo the thing yourself.
  • AI gives you wrong answers confidently, and you only catch that if you know the material yourself. So, please check.
